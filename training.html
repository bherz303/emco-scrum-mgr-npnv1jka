CTXH Manager Training

  1. The Scorecard Reference Sheet — what gets graded (your rubric)
  2. The Mock Practice Board — a broken board you'll fix as a hands-on exercise

Stage 1 · Day 1–2

Learn the Board

Understand what the board is, how work flows through it, and the vocabulary behind the scorecard.

Why we use a Scrum board

Your Monday board is your hammer and chisel. There is only so much time in a day, and the board is how we decide what actually gets worked, who owns it, and whether we're moving fast enough. A clean board isn't busywork — it's the difference between reacting to your week and running it.

Why this is a daily practice

Board hygiene isn't a once-a-week chore — it's daily. The goal is a board that's organized every day with the prioritized work you actually need to move, so you stay productive across the week on the numbers and projects you own.

The weekly rhythm:

  1. Monday: full plate. Today and This Week carry everything that needs attention for the week.
  2. Tuesday–Friday: work it down. Each day you knock things out, clearing Today and This Week item by item.
  3. By week's end: empty. Today and This Week are empty, and everything you touched has moved into Hold for Sync Up.
  4. Next Monday: sync and reset. That empty state is what lets you walk in, actually track last week's velocity and progress, get synced up, and start the next week clean.

Why it matters: skip this daily commitment and the board stops reflecting reality — you lose track of your own work. Left unchecked, that's not just messy, it's a failure in the role. Building the habit of daily organization is the real goal of this training; everything else in this module exists to support it.

The groups and how work flows

Work moves through groups in a predictable cycle. Learn this flow — most board problems come from items sitting in the wrong group.

  • Inbox — New/recurring, unorganized items land here. At sync or huddle: decide owner, next action, due date, priority. These get moved into your current work week, or deferred again if not a priority. The inbox should be checked and cleared daily. Nothing should sit in the inbox for more than 5 days — it is stale and mismanaged at that point.
  • Today — What you are working on today. If you're not touching it today, it doesn't belong here.
  • This Week — Work happening this week, each item carrying the projected day of focus. When that day arrives, you change the status to "Today" and it gets moved into your Today group. It's okay if you don't know the exact day of the week you need to work on it — simply put it for Wednesday, and when that day comes you can pick it up or push it to Thursday or Friday if it's not a priority yet.
  • Later / Deferred — Not this week. Every deferred item must carry a future Monday date — that triggers the automation to pull it back into Inbox before your next sync.
  • Hold for Sync Up — Where "Done" items for that week rest until reviewed at the next sync-up. Items here may be done for the week but may need to be picked back up the following week — that gets determined at the weekly sync-up meeting.
  • Done — Completed or sufficiently progressed task. This means it is fully done and you won't be picking it back up — the status should say "Synced."

This flow is automated, not manual. Changing an item's status is what moves it — you never drag a pulse between groups. Setting status to Today, This Week, Deferred, or Done automatically relocates the item, and status → Today also stamps today's date. Drag-and-drop bypasses this and is exactly what the Automations criterion is grading against.

Try it on the Mock Practice Board: duplicate the main board, rename it your test board. Then change any item's status and watch it jump groups on its own.

The Fibonacci LOW scale

We're bad at estimating time, so we use relative sizing instead. Daily Level of Work (DLOW) uses the Fibonacci scale: 1 = 15 min or less · 2 = 30–45 min · 3 = 1–2 hrs · 5 = 3–4 hrs · 8 = 5–6 hrs · 13 = 7–8 hrs · 21 = 9–12 hrs.

Daily LOW (DLOW) must always be one of these numbers. Weekly LOW (WLOW) is a cumulative total — it does not have to be Fibonacci. Target DLOW is 15–20 per person per day. If Today adds up to 40+, you're overloaded — you need to triage and reprioritize/reorganize. If you're sitting with an 8 DLOW, you haven't weighted the task correctly, or you're not filling your day with the appropriate amount of work.

QG Value, Velocity, and the Happiness pulse

  • QG Value — The point value on your Quarterly Goal pulses (your "big rocks"), usually matching Weekly LOW for that goal. Keeps your most important work visible above day-to-day noise.
  • Velocity — WLOW plus QG Value gives your pace for the week. Reviewed every sync.
  • Happiness pulse — Every team member should have one pulse that, when completed, makes them happy for the week. This typically should be tied to a critical task, a QG pulse, or something you'll be genuinely happy to get done and off your plate so you can get back to your big rocks. Visible in Today or This Week.
Example — a Velocity Check pulse's weekly update

Velocity = 88 (WLOW total)
QG Value = 20 (WLOW total for all QG pulses worked on that week)
Happiness = 5 (Very happy with work week)

  2. What's the difference between DLOW and WLOW?
  3. Which number needs to use the Fibonacci number, DLOW or WLOW?
  4. Why must deferred items have a future Monday date?
  5. What is your target DLOW per day?
Stage 2 · Day 3–5

Work the Board

Keep your board scoring Green against all 12 board hygiene criteria.

This stage maps directly to the scorecard. Each item below is what a grader looks for — learn to self-check before your board is ever graded.

The 10 Fundamentals (5 / 3 / 1 points each)

1. LOW Values — Every Today item has a valid Fibonacci DLOW.Self-check: scan Today — is any DLOW blank or non-Fibonacci (like 4 or 6)? One exception: the Velocity Check pulse is allowed DLOW 0 — it's a tracking pulse, not a worked task. That exception applies only to that pulse; a real task with DLOW 0 is still a miss. Target load is 15–20 per person per day.
2. Pulse Titles — Titles name the next action with enough context. Recognized shorthand ("ESP", "Dispatch", "Courtesy Texts") is fine.Self-check: would a teammate know what to do from the title alone?
3. Later Group Dates — Every deferred item has a future Monday date, and there is something in Later to begin with.Self-check: scroll to the Later group — any past dates or non-Mondays? And is it empty? An empty Later group is a miss: deferring nothing all week means work isn't being categorized — it's either riding in Today/This Week when it should be deferred, or getting dropped.
4. Happiness Pulse — Every manager on the board has one Happiness pulse identified, visible in Today or This Week.Self-check: do you have one, and does everyone else on the board? Board score: all managers have one = 5, some but not all = 3, none = 1. On your individual score it's simply pass/fail — you have one or you don't.
5. QG Pulses Visible & QG Value Set — Your Quarterly Goal "(Live)" pulses are in Today or This Week, and each has the QG Value column filled in.Self-check: are your QG pulses parked in Deferred/Later where nobody sees them? Any with a blank QG Value? Owning no QG pulses at all is a miss — every manager has Q3 rocks cascaded onto their board.
6. Inbox Age — Nothing untriaged older than 5 days.Self-check: review the Inbox group — anything stale? Check every item, not just the first one you spot. Freshly dropped items (today or yesterday) are not a violation, but may need attention. An empty Inbox is a full pass. A stale item is charged to whoever created it; unowned items go to the BM.
7. Updates with Context — Critical ongoing-project pulses (QG, truck/fleet repair and maintenance, sales, customer issues, employee tracking) carry a progress note posted within the last 7 days. Simple task placeholders don't need updates.Self-check: does each ongoing-project pulse have a note from this week on where it stands? Could someone else pick up this task without asking you for a status update? A months-old update doesn't count. Sub-items with real detail do. Damage claims are exempt. No current update but a title + Definition of Done that genuinely describes the action earns partial credit.
8. No Duplicates — One pulse per task. Archive or delete the extra copy — don't close it as Done.Closing a duplicate as Done leaves a false completion record while the live copy is still open — that scores 3, not 5. Note: multiple sales blocks on different days are intentional, not duplicates. Same-title/same-date pulses in Inbox tagged as a Delegation or DB Pulse are also legitimate — one copy lands per assignee before triage. That exception only holds while still in Inbox. When a duplicate spans two people, it's charged to whoever created the later copy.
9. Automations — Items move by status change, not drag-and-drop.Self-check: do your pulses' statuses and groups line up, or is something dragged into the wrong place? Board-level automation setup is the BM's responsibility and is scored on the BM alone — your individual score only asks whether your own pulses' statuses match their groups.
10. Definition of Done — The pulses you closed this week carry a real completion note saying what got accomplished.A real update earns full marks; a title + Definition of Done that clearly describes the work earns partial credit; neither is a miss. Only work closed since the last read is graded — old Done items aren't re-scored, and a cleanup sweep of long-closed pulses won't count against you. Closing nothing across a full week is a miss. Damage claims exempt.

The 2 Conscientious Details (5 / 3 / 1 points each — same scale, same weight)

11. Project Column — QG and DB Pulse items are always tagged; overall at least half the active board is tagged, so assign the project to the pulses where it makes sense.Self-check: are your QG and DB pulses labeled correctly, and do other pulses need their project assigned, like "Hiring," "Fleet," "Event Planning," etc.?
12. No Past-Due Items — Nothing in Today or This Week is past its due date.

These two are board craft rather than daily fundamentals. They are not optional, and as of v1.5 they are not scored harder either — the old 0-on-a-miss rule is gone.

Scoring reminder: Twelve criteria, 5 points each, all 60 counting toward one total. Every criterion scores 5, 3, or 1 — nothing scores 0, so the lowest possible board score is 12. A perfect board scores 60. The bar is 85% — 51 out of 60. Letter grades describe the read; the 85% line decides whether you pass it, and a B can land on either side of it.

The 5 highest-impact habits

  1. Set a LOW on every Today item before your sync.
  2. Tag the Project column on QG and DB pulses first, then the rest.
  3. Keep your Velocity pulse active — never mark it Done.
  4. Defer only to Monday dates.
  5. Post a one-line update on your critical project pulses so the board tells the story.
✅ Stage 2 checkpoint

Open your real board and self-score all 12 criteria. Bring your score to your Stage 3 walkthrough.

Mock Board Test-Out

This is how you prove you've actually learned the rubric, not just read it.

The scenario: treat this like the morning of your sync-up. Your board needs to be fixed and ready before the meeting — not something you're still working through while your manager is sitting across from you. Set it up like you're about to present it, not like you're doing homework with no deadline.

  1. Duplicate the Mock Practice Board — never work the master. Rename your copy your test board.
  2. Fix it. Work through it against the Reference Sheet and everything in this module — fix the real errors, leave the decoy patterns alone. Get it into presentable shape, the way you'd want it looking right before your RM sits down with you.
  3. Alert your Regional Manager once you believe it's fixed. Don't self-grade — the RM runs the scorecard.
  4. RM grades your duplicate. The master board starts at 29 out of 60 (48%, an F) — that's the fixed baseline every trainee is measured against.

To pass: you need 85 or higher, and your board has to show real improvement over that 29-point baseline — not just enough partial credit to clear 85 without actually fixing what mattered.

Once you're graded, your test copy gets deleted so the training board stays clean for the next person.

Stage 3 · Week 2

Lead the Sync

Run a sync-up that scores well on the Meeting Management criteria.

The sync-up agenda (in order)

  1. Velocity check — Review the team's pace. Happiness pulse is acknowledged here.
  2. Sort by priority — Critical on top, super-low at the bottom.
  3. Hold for Sync review — Focus on high-level and QG-value pulses. Don't cover every single item.
  4. Review This Week — Confirm dates, LOWs, and priorities.
  5. Review Today — Keep it tight and realistic.
  6. Inbox cleanup — Assign, date, prioritize, and clear.

Timing and flow

  • 5–10 min warm-up to connect and set the tone.
  • ~10 min per manager presenting their board.
  • 5–10 min for the BM to wrap Inbox and scorecard.
  • Rule of thumb: if a topic turns into a big discussion, table it and follow up 1:1 after the sync.
  • Present order: Field Manager → Flex Manager → Operations Manager → Branch Manager. Release FMs/Flex early if they need to get to on-sites.

What gets graded in a Full Eval (Meeting Management)

  • Pre-sync board prep done, RM alerted when ready
  • Agenda flow followed
  • Time kept to ~10 min per person
  • Big discussions tabled, followed up after
  • Tone engaged and constructive
  • Managers released early when appropriate
  • Velocity reviewed (with the Happiness pulse called out)
  • No drag-and-drop during the sync — status changes only
✅ Stage 3 checkpoint

Run a mock sync using the practice board. Your RM (or a peer) scores you on the 8 Meeting Management items. A passing certification is a Board Hygiene score of 90%+ plus a clean mock sync.

Certification

You're certified once you can:

  1. Pass all three stage checkpoints.
  2. Score your live board 90%+ on Board Hygiene two weeks running.
  3. Lead a mock sync that scores Green on the Meeting Management items.

Recertification runs on the same path whenever the rubric materially changes.

Staying Certified

Weekly Reads, Recovery & Re-test

Certification isn't a one-time badge. Here's how your board stays that way.

Mock certifies, live maintains

Initial certification is earned on your mock training board — 85% or better, with real improvement over your planted baseline. After that your real board is read once a week, typically on your sync day, so the score is fresh for the conversation. If the schedule shifts and it lands on a different day, it counts the same. You see it at the sync; it isn't posted to your board before you and your RM have talked.

Read bands: 51–60 is a pass · 45–50 is a soft miss · 12–44 is a miss.

One miss is not a pattern

A single read below the line gets a redirect logged for the team, and that's it. No recertification. Everybody has an off week, and one read tells you nothing about a habit.

What triggers recertification

  • Three misses inside any four reads — roughly three in a month.
  • Two reads in a row at 44 or below.

Same outcome; one path just gets there faster. A pass resets the count.

Recovery vs. Re-test — two different things

Recovery is the post-miss path: re-run your Stage 2 self-check against the reference sheet, fix what's flagged, then score 85%+ on two consecutive weekly reads. Recovery happens on your live board — the point is fixing the real thing, not re-passing a test.

Scheduled Re-test is separate: six months after your last passing test you re-test on the mock board. That one's a knowledge check.

Two more things worth knowing

  • BMs clear two bars — your own individual score and your board's score both have to clear 85%. A spotless personal board on a failing branch board isn't the job.
  • Time off doesn't count against you. If you're out for the read week, no read is logged at all — no miss, no pass, nothing on your ladder.

Escalation: hit a recertification trigger twice in the same quarter and the recovery isn't self-serve — your RM runs a live walkthrough with you instead of a remote read.
